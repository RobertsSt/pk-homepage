Student fraternities are not ordinary student societies held together only by a certain way of life. They are lifelong academic organisations that unite students of different courses and their older members who have completed their studies, the philisters. The absence of barriers between people of different ages, professions and standing, in an atmosphere of shared aims and ideals, is an essential and unique feature. From the moment he is admitted, a young student is an equal member of the society. The greatest advantage of a lifelong organisation is that the experience people have gathered does not leave with them but is handed on to the new members. The older members have always supported the younger ones, both by instilling the fraternity spirit and love of the fatherland and in purely everyday matters. This follows not only from the idea of brotherhood; it also has a history, from the time when not everyone could study and any help or advice was worth its weight in gold. The fraternities are academic organisations, and so particular attention and support is given to studies. Several fraternities or their members have set up scholarship funds that support the most talented and successful students, allowing them to give more of their attention to their studies and to activities in the convent, and so furthering education and intellectual growth. The scholarships are awarded in order to make the importance of fraternities and sororities better known among the students of Latvia’s universities and to stir interest in joining them, and they go to capable and diligent students of Latvia’s universities who, alongside their studies, take an active part in the life of Latvia’s fraternities and sororities.

**The scholarship of the student fraternity Lettonia**

The aim of the scholarship is to give financial support, and encouragement towards further academic and scholarly achievement, to diligent and talented students who alongside their studies are active and energetic members of the student fraternity Lettonia. The academic scholarships of the student fraternity Lettonia at the University of Latvia Foundation are financed by the Philisters’ Aid Society of the student fraternity Lettonia.\
The scholarship is intended for bachelor’s students (from the second year) and master’s students who are members of Lettonia.

**The scholarship of the Armīns Rūsis Scholarship Fund**

This scholarship is dedicated to the memory of Dr Armīns Rūsis, an outstanding lawyer and teacher both in the pre-war Republic of Latvia and abroad, and of his son Armīns I. Rūsis. It was founded to support capable and diligent Latvian students during their studies and to promote the academic unity of fraternities and sororities both during studies and beyond. The scholarship was set up in loving memory of his father and brother by Roberts Rūsis, a philister of Lettonia.\
The scholarship is intended for bachelor’s students (from the second year) and master’s students who are members of fraternities and sororities at these universities of the Republic of Latvia: the University of Latvia (LU), the Latvia University of Agriculture (LLU), Riga Technical University (RTU), Rīga Stradiņš University (RSU), the Stockholm School of Economics in Riga (REA) and the Latvian Maritime Academy (JA).

**The Vējiņš family scholarship**

In 2007 Jānis Rūdolfs Vējiņš, a Latvian living in Australia, founded scholarships named after Jānis and Aina Vējiņš for members of Latvia’s fraternities and sororities, with particular preference given to members of the student fraternity Gersicania.\
The scholarship is intended for bachelor’s students (from the second year) and master’s students who are members of Gersicania at the University of Latvia.

**The Alfrēds and Alma Apse scholarship**

This scholarship was founded in 2008 thanks to the support of the Canadian chapter of Fraternitas Lataviensis. Latvians living in Canada, valuing what the founding philister Alfrēds Apse and his wife Alma gave to the fraternity and honouring their memory, decided unanimously to create 11 scholarships in memory of Alfrēds and Alma, on condition that those who apply are studying members of the Fraternitas Lataviensis family in Latvia. The administration of the scholarships was entrusted to the Vītolu fonds foundation. Alfrēds Apse was one of the founders of Fraternitas Lataviensis. The money for the scholarship comes from the estate of Alfrēds and Alma Apse. The scholarship is used to support a studying member of Fraternitas Lataviensis as well as other students of limited means, chosen by the Vītolu fonds.

And that is not all: many fraternities have internal scholarships of their own, whose size and terms are an internal matter and are not open to the wider public.
