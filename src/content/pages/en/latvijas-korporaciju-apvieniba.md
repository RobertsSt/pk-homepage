After the war many Latvians who had left the land of their fathers under the threat of war found refuge in Germany, in the displaced persons camp at Esslingen. Among them were several hundred members of fraternities. At first they were far less active than before. Some gathered in the Muchov beer cellar, registered with the police as an old men’s club. The members of this circle were Riga fraternity men of many semesters, whose minds were not only on memories of days gone by. They looked to the future and debated how the interrupted work of the fraternities could best be restored. A step in this direction was the first commers of the nations, organised on 25 May 1948 on the premises of the Esslingen sports club. Eighteen fraternities with 151 members took part in the commers.

During the commers representatives of the fraternities met to exchange views on restoring the work of P!K!. Those present agreed that in exile the work of P!K! could not be restored strictly as it had been in Riga, and that a substitute organisation had to be founded, with statutes of its own suited to life in exile. The drafting of the statutes of the Association of Latvian Fraternities was entrusted to a board of seven men. On 5 March 1949 the board called a meeting of the fraternities’ representatives, in which 11 fraternities took part, representing 18 of the 32 votes of P!K!.

The representatives adopted the statutes of L!K!A! as drafted, elected the first presidium and chose Esslingen as the temporary seat of the association. The statutes laid down that the aim of L!K!A! is to coordinate the work of the fraternities united in it, founded on the members’ ideal of Latvia’s freedom, on love of the fatherland, on the fraternity spirit and on the basic principles of P!K!. As emigration grew, the convent of L!K!A! decided to move the seat of the association to New York in America and to elect the presidium from among the fraternity members living there.

The first convent of L!K!A! met in New York on 23 September 1950. Only the representatives of 10 fraternities took part. Emigration had left its mark on the work of the fraternities. It had to be built up and fitted to the peculiar scattering of their members. Officers had to be found who had the gifts and the time to carry out the many tasks of reorganisation. Restoring the work overseas was not the task of a day; it took great effort and time. With each successive convent further fraternities announced that they had resumed their work. By 1954 the period of organisation was over, and all 21 fraternities of P!K!, as well as 4 fraternities founded in Germany, had joined in the work of the L!K!A! convent.\
By September 1989, 157 convents and 1 seniors’ convent had been held in New York. The presidia of L!K!A! and of the fraternities have travelled to the convents from Boston, Chicago, Delaware, New Jersey, Washington and even from Toronto in Canada. L!K!A! is now incorporated and exempt from income tax.

The work of L!K!A! is governed by its statutes, comments and decisions and by the comments of P!K!. Those who drew up the statutes of the association tried to take over from the earlier comments of the Riga period only what suited life in exile, and for the rest to look for new forms and institutions. In later years the statutes of L!K!A! were revised and supplemented (L!K!A! minutes 97, 98, 173, 175). The highest governing body of L!K!A! is the convent, made up of the presidia of the fraternities or their representatives. The fraternities’ voting rights depend on the number of their members: up to 100 members, 1 vote; up to 200 members, 2 votes; and over 200 members, 3 votes. The decisions of the convent are binding on all the fraternities and their members. The conduct of the convent and the order of voting are laid down in the Comment of Procedure and Order of the L!K!A! Convent (112). L!K!A! is represented, and the carrying out of its decisions supervised, by the presidium of L!K!A!. In the first years of L!K!A! the presidium was elected by the convent. Only in 1966 (66) did the convent, recognising that the work of the fraternities had become firm enough for their presidia to take on presiding, decide to return to the order of Riga. Presiding in the fraternities’ order of seniority works well. Fraternitas Arctica, Salgalia, Lacuania and Ruthenia have declined to preside.

In Riga only a student of the University of Latvia could be a member of a fraternity. The philisters were united in philisters’ societies. Had the order of Riga been kept, the small number of students would have deprived the fraternities of every chance to work. The statutes of L!K!A! therefore provided for the philisters to be brought into the active body of the fraternities. The admission of new members is governed by the Rules on the Suitability of Students for Admission to Fraternities (67, 68). Students may be admitted who study at generally recognised institutions and whose course of study leads to an academic degree. The names of those newly admitted are to be reported to L!K!A!. Their qualification is examined by the officer for study affairs, and depending on his finding L!K!A! accepts or rejects the admission. Admitting an honorary philister requires the consent of L!K!A!.

A peculiar institution of fraternity life, without any precedent, is the K!K!. In the opinion of the L!K!A! convent, “fraternity group” (korporāciju kopa, K!K!) is a generic name to be used for this new institution of fraternity life in exile, together with the name of the K!K! in question. The work of the K!K! is governed by the Standard Statutes of the K!K! (93, 95, 153). The K!K! are formed on the principle of free will, and only members of the fraternities united in L!K!A! can be their members. The highest governing body of a K!K! is its convent. In matters of the courts a K!K! has wide jurisdiction over its members. The K!K! have grown into strong units of fraternity life, in which members of all the fraternities, in cooperation and tolerance, cultivate the principles of fraternity life with very good results. Forty-six recognised K!K! are active within L!K!A!.

The Comment of Honour of L!K!A! binds all members of the fraternities (88, 89). No fraternity member may refuse to comply with it. Unlike the comment of P!K!, the Comment of Honour gives no choice between an apology and the weapons of the fraternities. The only form of satisfaction it provides for is an apology. Where an act has been committed, a settlement is not permitted. All minutes of a court of honour (G!T!) must be sent to the competent fraternity court, which punishes the guilty party if it finds breaches of fraternity ethics.

The punishment of fraternity members in external matters is laid down in the Comment of Court Proceedings (93, 95, 127, 130). Within L!K!A! there are the Supreme Fraternity Court (Augstākā Buršu tiesa, A!B!T!) and the Central Fraternity Court (C!B!T!), and within each K!K! a Fraternity Court (B!T!). A B!T! hears cases concerning the members of its own group; the C!B!T! hears cases between members of different groups or of members who belong to no group; and the A!B!T! hears appeals against the decisions of the B!T! and the C!B!T!. The judges of the C!B!T! are elected by the convent of L!K!A!, and the judges of a B!T! by the convent of the K!K!. The judges of the A!B!T! are chosen by the fraternities. The heaviest penalty is expulsion from the fraternity family for 99 years.

To support Latvian national life and political aims, the Fatherland Fund (Tēvijas fonds) exists and works within L!K!A! (118). Its means come from the voluntary donations of members of fraternities and sororities. The distribution of the donations is approved by the convent of L!K!A!. By September 1989 more than $64,000 had come into the Fatherland Fund and been distributed. In truth the fraternities’ contribution is far greater, for many fraternities donate very considerable sums on their own occasions without going through the fund. The largest single gift was a donation of $55,000.

L!K!A! has also been active as a publisher. To throw light on the situation and the stance of the Latvian people under the two occupying powers, L!K!A! published, while still in Germany, “The Baltic Nations Want to Live” in English. Emigration to countries overseas began and the venture ran dry; nor did the book “Sport of the Presidium Convent”, published in later years, meet with success, and its unsold copies were divided among the fraternities in proportion to their contributions. The song book “Dziesmas buršu saimei” (Songs for the Fraternity Family), by contrast, has been very successful. Both editions, with print runs of 2,000 and 3,500, are sold out. Equally successful was the publication in colour, issued in 1966, on the fraternities of L!K!A! and their history. This single edition of 5,787 copies is also sold out. In 1954 L!K!A! revived “Universitas”, once published in Riga, which now appears twice a year in a print run of 5,200. “Universitas” has grown into the most widely printed academic Latvian publication in the free world, whose many-sided and broad contents reflect the life and thought of the fraternities. “Universitas” is a joint undertaking of L!K!A! and S!P!K!, the convent of the sororities, and subscribing to it is compulsory for the members of all fraternities. By the end of 1989, 63 issues of “Universitas” had appeared in the free world.

The work of L!K!A! over the past 40 years has been very active and many-sided, and has made L!K!A! the largest and strongest organisation of the academic family in the Western world, uniting 3,800 members. Members of the fraternities, working in the central Latvian organisations, in congregations and in cultural life, have done great national and political work towards regaining Latvia’s freedom. Assimilation and the long years abroad have not passed the fraternities by either, and their membership has shrunk in recent years. New members no longer make up for the losses. The circle of life of the older generation, which built and shaped L!K!A!, is closing fast. The middle and younger generations it brought up have taken over the leadership of the fraternities and of L!K!A! and are carrying on the well-begun work with notable success. Let us wish them the ardour and idealism of the earlier generations in carrying onward and guarding the tricolours of the fraternities. The primary aim of L!K!A!, the restoration of the legal standing of the Latvian state, has not yet been achieved.
