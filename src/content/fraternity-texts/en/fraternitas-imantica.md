title: Fraternitas Imantica
translatedFrom: 2024-05-03T18:38:04

On 14 March 1946 the Baltic University began its work in Hamburg, Germany. For the Latvian students learning alone was not enough. They lacked an organisation that would unite them in friendship and bring them up for public life. Word went round that the fraternities of L!U!P!K! (the Presidium Convent of the University of Latvia) would not be admitting new members. When the Baltic University moved to Pinneberg, a United Convent was formed there, and its members spread the ideas of the fraternities among the students who belonged to no organisation. The need for student organisations had so ripened that on 18 February 1947, 24 students of the Baltic University founded a new Latvian student fraternity, Fraternitas Imantica.

The founders, who had grown up in the independent Latvian state, had seen it flourish in all its splendour and had fought for its liberation in the Second World War, wished to give their abilities and energy in future, too, to regaining Latvia. The choice of the fraternity’s name and colours was governed by the region of Vidzeme and by the situation of our people at the time of the founding. The meaning of the colours: red for love of the fatherland, gold for readiness to make sacrifices for Latvia, white for education and ardour. The coetus of the founders was brought up by Professor Jānis Muižnieks, a philister of Selonija.

The fraternity vouched for the A!K!K! with Selonija on 18 February 1948. Contact with the family of Latvian fraternity members, with the members of the United Convent of the Baltic University, helped the new fraternity to grow in a fairly short time into traditions cultivated over decades.

Fraternitas Imantica was founded with the colours red, white and gold. Lettonia objected to this arrangement of colours, pointing out that the members of Lettonia had worn these colours in Tartu and that the presidium of Lettonia still used them. The colours were changed to red, gold and white, and with this arrangement Fraternitas Imantica was admitted to the Association of Latvian Fraternities on 16 September 1949.

By the time the Baltic University closed on 30 September 1949, 6 coetus of new members had been admitted and Fraternitas Imantica had 57 members, but the active work of the convent had to be suspended, and guidelines were adopted for its work in further emigration. At the universities of the free world the members of Imantica resumed their interrupted studies and founded internal chapters, and in 1952 they began to bring up new members again. The present total of coetus of new members is 40. Chapters have been active in Wisconsin, in Europe and in Australia and New Zealand. In 1972 new guidelines for global work, suited to the circumstances, were adopted. Fraternitas Imantica presided over L!K!A! in the academic years 1957/58 and 1975/76. Fraternitas Imantica now has 112 members.
