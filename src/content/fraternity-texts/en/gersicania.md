Amicus amico, omnia patriae: these words were laid at the foundation of the convent when the fraternity was founded, when a group of young students, studying at their school in exile, the Baltic University, came together to create the Latvian student fraternity Gersicania. The founding day of Gersicania is 14 March 1947, when its 24 founders signed the solemn pledge in Pinneberg.

The name Gersicania is taken from the castle of Gersika on the bank of the Daugava, mentioned in the Chronicle of Henry. The colours of Gersicania stand for: white, for learning and a man’s honour; violet, for ardour for the homeland; green, for friendship and the fraternity spirit. On 24 February 1948 Gersicania vouched for the A!K!K! with Selonija. On 16 September 1949, in Esslingen, Gersicania was admitted to L!K!A!.

The Baltic University closed on 1 October 1949. With that Gersicania went over to statutes for exile and suspended its active life for a time. The active life of the convent was restored in the spring of 1953, when new members were admitted in New York, and so the fraternity fulfilled its duty to gather the young Latvians who were studying and to bring them up for the fraternity family and for Latvia.

In the years that followed, much work and effort went into organising the separate chapters and shaping their internal life. As a result ten chapters were formed in Europe, Australia, Canada and the USA, uniting more than 200 members of the Gersicania family.

After the Baltic University closed, studies had to be broken off, but once they had arrived in the new countries of exile most members of Gersicania took them up again. The most favourable countries for resuming studies were the USA and Canada, where the level of the economy soon made it possible to earn the means to continue. It was harder in Australia, where on arrival one was bound by a two-year work contract. For all that, the members of Gersicania who reached Australia also gradually completed their studies.

Since the second half of 1949 the fraternity Gersicania has published its own internal periodical, Gersicanias Apkārtraksts (the Gersicania Circular). In the course of time the contents of the Circular have grown and it has become a journal, of which more than a hundred issues have now appeared. It has proved its necessity, having become almost the only link between the presidium and the individual members of the convent in different countries.

The Philisters’ Fund of Gersicania has existed since 1979.

With a donation of $6,000 the fraternity took part in building Dzintari, the Latvian educational centre in Australia, where on the opening day, 17 January 1988, one dormitory was named after Gersicania and a plaque was fixed to it.

Gersicania presided over L!K!A! in 1958/59 and 1976/77.
