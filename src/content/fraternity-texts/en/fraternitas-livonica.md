title: Fraternitas Livonica
translatedFrom: 2024-05-03T18:39:05
---

Fraternitas Livonica was founded on 29 October 1926 in Riga, at the University of Latvia. Its founders came from among the members of the student society Austrums, who by the end of 1925 had begun to look on the fraternities with sympathy. They were impressed by the fraternities’ firmness, experience and customs, their strict order, their internal life built on warmth and friendship, and equally by their unbending stance for nation and state. These new ideas were discussed at the general meeting of Austrums on 3 March 1926, attended by a large number of its old members. At a stormy meeting the proposal to turn Austrums into a fraternity was rejected by 63 votes for and 63 against. After this meeting 47 members left Austrums and founded Fraternitas Livonica.

The new fraternity chose the colours light blue, violet and gold: light blue for clearness of heart, violet for striving after achievement and gold for the gathering of spiritual values. The mottos adopted were “Lūzti, bet neliecies” (Break, but do not bend) and “Vīrs un vārds” (A man and his word). They reminded the members of the new fraternity to keep their word as men with honour and to go through life as fighters, letting no winds of life blow them off course. The new fraternity vouched for the Comment of the United Fraternities with Lettonia on 7 December 1926, and on 11 December the internal convent at which the colours were first worn was held. A year later, on 17 December 1927, a philisters’ society was founded, which bought a house for the convent: the White House by Viesturs Garden, at Veru iela 6. On 20 November 1928 the fraternity was admitted as a full member of the Presidium Convent. In 1931 the fraternity consecrated in its house a coat of arms made by the artist Hugo Mercs and a flag given by Dr Alfrēds Bīlmanis. Fraternitas Livonica concluded a cartel with the Lithuanian fraternity Fraternitas Baltiensis. Fraternitas Livonica presided over the Presidium Convent twice: in 1932, when the 6 Baltic German fraternities left P!K!, and in 1940, when the Russians invaded Latvia and the work of P!K! and all the fraternities was officially closed. In the time of free Latvia 254 students of the University of Latvia joined the convent in all; 20 of them had taken part in Latvia’s War of Independence.

The Russian year in Latvia and the Second World War cut deep gaps in the ranks of the convent. More than 20 members of the convent were deported to Siberia, and 9 of the 47 founders died in deportation. More than 10 members fell in the Second World War. In honour of the 37 members of its family who fell in the Second World War or were lost in the Year of Terror and the storms of war, the convent unveiled a memorial plaque to its heroes and martyrs at a solemn ceremony at the Brothers’ Cemetery in the Catskills in October 1984.

When the war ended there were 100 members of Livonica in the free world. They began to get in touch with one another, and talks started about resuming the work of the convent. The work truly resumed, however, only in the early 1950s, when chapters were founded in Australia, Chicago, New York, New Zealand and Europe.

At the beginning of 1988, 7 of the 47 founders of the convent were still alive. In Latvia 35 members of Livonica had been traced. Of the 100 who went into exile, exactly 35 were likewise still alive. The number admitted in the free world already exceeds 100, and together there are about 130 members. More than 20 of them are already second-generation members of Livonica. The largest number is in the USA, where the globally elected presidium also has its seat.
