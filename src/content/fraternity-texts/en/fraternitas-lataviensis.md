The 28 founders of Fraternitas Lataviensis included a group of graduates of the Vilis Olavs Commercial School and a fairly large number of members of the temperance movement. The fraternity vouched for the A!K!K! with Lettonia.

The name of the fraternity honours an ancient centre of Latvian culture that lay in the old valley of the Daugava, in the district of Krustpils. The motto expresses the ideals and principles of the founders and their promise to devote themselves to the fatherland, to justice and to the sense of honour. When the new fraternity was admitted to the Presidium Convent on 20 November 1928, its original name, Latavia, and its original colours, black, blue and gold, were changed.

In the spring of 1927 the Philisters’ Society of Fraternitas Lataviensis was founded; there was also a Ladies’ Committee of Fraternitas Lataviensis. In 1937 a plot of land was bought at Šarlotes iela 9 and plans were drawn up for building a house of its own. The convent presided over P!K! in the 1st semester of 1931 and the 1st semester of 1939.

The active and purposeful work of the convent was cut short by the occupation of our country. It, and the war that followed, struck deep wounds in the brotherhood of the convent.

Ninety-eight members of Lataviensis reached freedom. In 1951, as the convent celebrated its 25th anniversary, the first new members were admitted abroad. By now 484 brothers have joined the family of Fraternitas Lataviensis. Chapters are active in Australia, the USA, Canada, Germany and Sweden. The main centre of activity is in Toronto, where the convent quarters have been set up and the convent’s library is kept. The successes of Fraternitas Lataviensis in the annual basketball and volleyball tournaments of L!K!A! deserve mention. The present number of members is around 200.

Since 1927 literary evenings have held an important place in the work of Fraternitas Lataviensis. In the beginning their liveliness can be explained by the fact that in the homeland the convent had many schoolteachers, writers and promoters of the temperance movement. Today the literary evenings deepen interest in our mother tongue and develop the ability to set out one’s view in writing and then to defend it in the debate that follows the paper. A paper by the new member is still required before he receives his colours. An internal journal, Goda Prātam, appears regularly. For its fiftieth anniversary the convent published a book of its history, Fraternitas Lataviensis 1926–1976.

Fraternitas Lataviensis presided over L!K!A! in 1971/72.
