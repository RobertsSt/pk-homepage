The fraternity Latvia was founded in Tartu as the first Latvian fraternity at the Veterinary Institute of Tartu (TVI). Its first colours were red, green and gold, and its motto Concordia Res Parvae Crescunt. On admission to the Presidium Convent the colours and mottos were changed to the present ones.

The aims of the fraternity Latvia are to unite Latvians with an academic education, to maintain Latvian student traditions, to guard the freedom and independence of the Latvian people and the Latvian state, and to increase the spiritual strength of the Latvian people. The meaning of the colours: red for the land of our fathers, blue for friendship and virtue, and gold for justice and a man’s honour.

After the Latvian state was proclaimed in 1918, all the founders and members took part in the War of Independence, and for that reason the fraternity suspended its work for a time. Being in Estonia, the great majority served in the North Latvian Brigade; only a few reached the western front of the new state, serving in the Student Company and the Cēsis Company. The work of the convent was resumed in Riga, at the University of Latvia, in 1920, and on 19 April 1921 Latvia was admitted to the Presidium Convent (P!K!). In 1926 a Philisters’ Aid Society was founded, which in 1930 bought the convent’s house at Šarlotes iela 3.

Although in Tartu the fraternity Latvia admitted only students of veterinary medicine, in Riga its members could come from all faculties. By 1940, when the communist and Russian occupying power put an end to its work, 387 students had been admitted, 20 of whom had already begun their studies in Tartu. During the German occupation only the Philisters’ Society was active, holding a few commerses. In exile the first gatherings were in Sweden in 1945 and then in Esslingen, Germany. The work was fully restored in Esslingen in 1948. Since then 232 students have been admitted in exile, and 619 in the whole time of the fraternity’s existence. Of the 208 members of the present active family, more than two thirds were admitted in exile. Of the living semesters of Tartu and Riga, 46 live in Latvia.

In 1951 the global leadership was moved from Esslingen to Toronto, Canada. In some years the global presidium was in New York and Boston, USA. The fraternity has presided over P!K! 3 times and over L!K!A! twice. The largest chapter has formed in Toronto. Chapters and groups are active in Sweden; in Australia, in Sydney and Perth; in the USA, in Boston, Chicago/Milwaukee, Minneapolis, Cleveland, Lincoln, Washington/Oregon, Indiana and California; and in Canada, in Toronto, Ottawa and Calgary. Members living on their own are to be found in Germany, Denmark and Brazil, and earlier also in England, Argentina, Afghanistan and Chile.

In the convent quarters there is a memorial plaque, VIŅI IR AR MUMS (They are with us), in honour of the 39 members of Latvia who fell in the war, were tormented to death in the deportations or were murdered by the occupying power. Eighty-two members served in the Latvian army in the War of Independence, at least 80 in the Legion and about 10 in Korea and Vietnam. Members have served in British and American guard units and in the armed forces of Australia, the USA and Canada, and, caught up in the whirl of war, even in the occupiers’ People’s Army and Red Army.

The first flag of the fraternity Latvia, preserved through the Year of Terror, is now in the convent quarters in Toronto. The quarters are in the fraternity’s own house near the University of Toronto, at 227 Beverley Street. The house was bought in 1980 by Šarlotes Nams Limited, a joint-stock company of the members of the fraternity Latvia.

Since 1950 the fraternity has published an internal journal, Pro Latvia, and, since its Riga days, Pindzele, a satirical publication by the new members. To mark its 50th anniversary it published an album and history, Latvia 1917–1967, and for its 60th anniversary a similar one, Latvia Trimdā (Latvia in Exile). The Internal Comment was renewed in 1972. The original Beer Comment has been preserved from the Riga days. A Self-Help Fund and a Scholarship Fund are at work. The fraternity Latvia is a participant in the Freedom Fund and a shareholder in Garezers and the Toronto Latvian Centre, and has given money towards publishing the textbook for the 4th year of primary school, Kas Darīja? Pats Darīja, and the planned new Hymnal of the Evangelical Lutheran Church.
