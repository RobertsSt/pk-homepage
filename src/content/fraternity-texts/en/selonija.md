## Pastimes

![Couples in evening dress dancing in a ballroom with balconies](@/assets/fraternities/selonija/photo-2.jpg)

Every week the members of Selonija have a range of activities: fencing practice, basketball and football training, ballroom dancing lessons. Selonija’s running, cycling and chess clubs are active, and in summer its yacht club. Besides this, members take on impressive challenges of their own, such as climbing Everest, skiing across Antarctica or running round Mont Blanc.

## History

![A historical black-and-white group photograph: several hundred members of the fraternity in front of a two-storey house](@/assets/fraternities/selonija/photo-3.jpg)

The commers for the 50th anniversary of the founding, Stabu iela, 1930.

Selonija is the first Latvian student fraternity, founded at the Riga Polytechnic Institute on 24 November 1880, when its 25 founders submitted their application for admission to the Chargierten-Convent (C!C!). The founders of Selonija were members of the Circle of Friends founded in 1878 by the student Klāvs Pūriņš, who were joined in 1879 by 19 fraternity members who had left the German fraternities Fraternitas Baltica, Concordia Rigensis and Rubonia, and also Borysthenia.

Of the 6 fraternities that made up the C!C!, the 3 German ones voted against admitting Selonija, so as to keep their commanding position over the non-German fraternities. Defending their own honour and that of the Latvian people, the members of Selonija fought several hundred hard and bloody Mensur bouts with members of the German fraternities.

Only on 24 November 1897 was Selonija admitted to the C!C!.

For the 25th anniversary the SELONIJAS ALBUMS, 1880–1905 was published, containing the biographies of 314 members of Selonija with photographs and pictures of the coetus. In 1909 the Aid Society of Selonija was founded, which gathered the philisters of Selonija. In the autumn of 1915 Selonija too was evacuated to Moscow together with the Riga Polytechnic Institute and worked there until the autumn of 1917.

In the autumn of 1918 the German authorities reopened the former Polytechnic Institute under the name Baltische Technische Hochschule, where a considerable number of commilitones continued their studies, and the convent of Selonija resumed its work in Riga.

- On 18 November 1918 the members of Selonija took part in the ceremony proclaiming the Latvian state. The next day the new members of Selonija raised the flags of Latvia at the main entrance of the old building of the university.
- On 29 November the General Meeting of Selonija resolved unanimously that all active commilitones would place themselves at the disposal of the Minister of Defence. In the War of Independence the members of Selonija are linked above all with the course of the Separate Student Company of Kalpaks’ battalion. The soldiers of the Student Company elected a member of Selonija, Captain Nikolajs Grundmanis, as their first commander. For bravery in Latvia’s War of Independence 40 members of Selonija were awarded the military Order of Lāčplēsis.
- Selonija took part in founding the Presidium Convent.
- Selonija acquired a two-storey house at Stabu iela 17, with the convent’s rooms on the first floor and the Philisters’ Society on the second. In the half-basement a room for social life was built, with a small stage for the theatre of the new members. Selonija had a symphony orchestra of 30 players.
- The Philisters’ Aid Society of Selonija (S!F!P!B!) bought a section for Selonija in the Forest Cemetery, including the resting place of the philister Jānis Poruks with its monument.
- At a meeting on 31 August in Esslingen, Germany, the United Convent of Selonija was founded, which took over the leadership of Selonija in exile. It was made up of philisters and active members of the convent. Nineteen chapters of Selonija are active in the free world. At the Brothers’ Cemetery in the Catskills, in the state of New York, there is a section for Selonija with places for 120 urns.

Selonija concluded a cartel of friendship with Lettonia in 1907, and in 1924 with Vironia, the Estonian fraternity founded in Riga and later of the University of Tartu.

Selonija publishes the journals DRAUGAM TĒVIJAI and SELONIJA. Fraternitas Vesthardiana, Vendia, Salgalia, Fraternitas Imantica, Gersicania and Fraternitas Vanenica vouched for the comment with Selonija.
