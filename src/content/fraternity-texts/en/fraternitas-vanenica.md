Uncertainty had not left that part of the Latvian people which found itself abroad, nor had fears for the future been dispelled, when young Latvians on the refugee road met in the capital of Bavaria to gain knowledge at the universities of Munich.

Scourged by what they had lived through together and called to unity, 18 of them, for the most part students of the University of Latvia, gathered the academic youth, so that it should not be lost to the Latvian nation, to shape its life abroad, with this call:

Conscious of our mission not to tire in the fight for our people and our homeland on the Baltic Sea, we undertake to increase knowledge and the strength of the spirit, to preserve ourselves for the community of the nation, and to grow in the spirit of the fraternities of the Presidium Convent of the University of Latvia, strengthening love of the fatherland, friendship and the sense of duty.

Latvian students from Kurzeme, Vidzeme, Zemgale and Latgale, at the universities of Munich at the foot of the Alps, gathered on 20 June 1947 on the premises of the Latvian Committee of Bavaria at Herzog-Rudolf-Straße 49 and founded Fraternitas Vanenica, the fraternity of the Latvian students of the universities of Munich, with the mottos “Tēvijai, pienākumam, draudzībai” (For fatherland, duty and friendship) and “Veritati, humanitati, virtuti”, and with the colours green, red and silver, which were to unite them not only during their studies but for life.

The name Vanenica is taken from ancient Kursa, which in the 12th century was divided into districts. In its north, washed by the Baltic Sea, lay Vanēnija. This geographical setting shaped the colours of our tricolour: green is the vital strength of that setting, while red and silver are taken from the arms of Kurzeme.

Fraternitas Vanenica vouched for the A!K!K! with Selonija on 6 September 1947, and on 16 September 1949 it was admitted to the Association of Latvian Fraternities.

On 21 June 1950 the convent of Fraternitas Vanenica resumed its work in the USA, with the seat of its presidium in New York.

The largest chapter of the convent was active in Caracas, Venezuela.

A Ladies’ Committee worked alongside the fraternity.

On 16 February 1951 L!K!A! granted the convent the right to vote.

The convent has an Aid Fund and a library, in which publications concerning the fraternities are collected.

The members of Vanenica have been active not only in internal life; externally, too, they have served society, national causes and the community of fraternity members.

Number of members: 60. The convent of Fraternitas Vanenica presided over the Association of Latvian Fraternities in the academic years 1959/60 and 1978/79.
