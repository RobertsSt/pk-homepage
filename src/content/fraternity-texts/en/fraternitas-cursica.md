Fraternitas Cursica was founded on 7 May 1947 in Pinneberg, Germany, at the Baltic University, where about 1,500 Latvians were studying at the time. As early as 29 April 1947 its 27 founders elected a provisional presidium and chose the colours black, blue and gold, a motto and the name of the fraternity. Among the founders were several oarsmen of the club Universitātes Sports (US), who said that the Kurši (the Curonians) had been brave men and fine oarsmen, and so the name of the fraternity arose: the Brotherhood of the Kurši, or Fraternitas Cursica. The oarsmen justified the chosen name with several victories of the Fraternitas Cursica crew at international rowing regattas in Hamburg.

On 7 May 1947 Fraternitas Cursica held its first commers and chose that date as its founding day. At one of the next convents the colours were rearranged to black, gold and blue, the custom of Tartu was adopted, and the fraternity vouched for the A!K!K! with Lettonia. On 15 September 1949 Fraternitas Cursica was admitted to L!K!A! and uncovered its colours. When the Baltic University closed in October 1949, Fraternitas Cursica had already held 130 convents and 62 literary evenings and brought up 6 coetus of new members. Many of its members left Germany, but the work of the convent went on under a special comment, uniting its members in six different countries. The largest centres of the Kurši are in Sydney and Toronto. The total number of members has already reached 85.

Every two years a presiding chapter is elected. Its special convent has worked on a global scale in both Toronto and Sydney. The common life of the Kurši in Europe is coordinated by a specially elected representative.

In 1981 the members of the Toronto chapter bought a house of their own, Kuršu joma, where after thorough rebuilding the convent quarters were also set up.

The colours of the flag of Fraternitas Cursica stand for the three parts of its motto: black for deeds for justice, gold for the sword for the fatherland, and blue for loyalty to the friend.

On its round anniversaries Fraternitas Cursica publishes an illustrated journal, Kuršu Vēstis. Number of members: 105.
