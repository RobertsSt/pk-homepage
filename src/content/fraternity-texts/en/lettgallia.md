On 8 February 1899 nine Latvian students of the Institute of Pharmacy of the University of Tartu founded the third oldest Latvian student fraternity, under the name Lettgallia, as the territory inhabited by the Latvians is called in Latin in the old chronicles. The symbolism of the fraternity’s colours is expressed in the song of its flag: green, overcome all doubt; black, let work go on in earnest; white, let a pure heart beat in the breast. The Internal Comment drawn up by the founders, which also contained the Comment of Honour, and the Beer Comment are in their main lines still in force today. Lettgallia received permission to uncover its colours on 10 January 1912.

Until the 2nd semester of 1918 Lettgallia worked in Tartu; then it moved its work to Riga, and its colours, which had been forbidden during the war, were uncovered anew at the founding ceremony of the University of Latvia on 28 September 1919. Since the 2nd semester of 1920 the fraternity has admitted students of all faculties. The work of the convent, cut short by the occupying power in the summer of 1940, was restored on 23 February 1946 in Esslingen, Germany, and in the 1st semester of 1948 the first new member in exile was admitted. The work of the convent goes on in 14 chapters in Europe, the USA, Canada, Australia and New Zealand and is coordinated by the globally elected presiding convent of Lettgallia, which from 1951 to 1971 was in New York. Since 1971 the functions of the presiding convent have been carried out in rotation by the largest chapters. In 89 years more than 800 members have been admitted to Lettgallia, 508 of them before the work of the convent was interrupted in Latvia.

In 1901 the Philisters’ Aid Society of Lettgallia was founded, which later renamed itself the Philisters’ Society of Lettgallia. It awarded its first scholarship in 1904. In 1927 a house was bought in Riga, at Dzirnavu iela 36, where the active convent lived. In the Forest Cemetery a burial mound was bought and tended, where the members of Lettgallia who died in Riga are laid to rest. On 22 February 1947 the Philisters’ Society united, until further notice, with the active convent. In 1921 the Ladies’ Committee of Lettgallia was founded, which is still at work.

In 1919 the members of Lettgallia took part in Latvia’s War of Independence, and Lettgallia was one of the five fraternities that in turn kept the flag of the Student Company of Kalpaks’ battalion. To the members of Lettgallia who lost their lives for Latvia during the occupation and the Second World War, the convent unveiled a memorial plaque on 2 June 1979 at the Brothers’ Cemetery in Elka Park, in the state of New York. A section of the cemetery has also been set aside for members of Lettgallia who have died in North America and for their families.

On 27 September 1919 Lettgallia, together with 4 other Latvian fraternities, took part in founding the Presidium Convent, and on 28 May 1948 in founding the Association of Latvian Fraternities. Lettgallia first presided over P!K! in the 2nd semester of 1921 and over L!K!A! in 1962/63.

Since the founding, literary evenings have held a notable place in the life of Lettgallia; besides the papers required of the new members, philisters of its own and of other convents have spoken there on current national, public and scholarly themes. Since the days of Tartu the new members have read out their satirical journal, Šmolis, at commerses.

At the very beginning of its work the convent set up a library, which in 1939 held several thousand volumes. In 1926 the convent published the Lettgallia song book, and in 1959 a collection of writings, Lettgallia 60 gados (Lettgallia in 60 Years). Since 1961 an internal journal, Fidelitas, has appeared. An album of the members of Lettgallia is in preparation.

Since 1951 the convent has had a Funeral Fund, an Aid Fund and a Scholarship Fund.

Fraternitas Metropolitana and Ruthenia vouched for the comment with Lettgallia. On 17 May 1975 Lettgallia concluded a cartel with the Estonian fraternity Rotalia.

In Latvia its work was restored in 1989.
