## About Ventonia

Founded – 21 November 1917\
Active members – 152

Student fraternities are lifelong academic organisations that admit only students who are studying. Historically it has come about that all fraternities are closed organisations, Ventonia among them. This is bound up with student life and its independence of any political regime. In this the fraternities differ essentially from other lifelong academic organisations.

Ventonia is at present the fourth largest student fraternity in Latvia. Its activities consist of various internal events (commerses, ladies’ evenings, literary evenings, fencing practice, sports and so on) and of external literary evenings with talks by people well known in Latvia (for example A. Dambis, J. Venters, O. Spurdziņš and others). The celebration of the days of national importance, 11 and 18 November, has a special place.

The link with the Latvia University of Agriculture (LLU) in Jelgava was formed in 1990, when Ventonia officially resumed its work in Latvia, because several of the surviving philisters were then living in Jelgava. Many members of Ventonia have taken an active part in the student self-governments and so helped to shape student life at LLU. Since 1990 more than 100 members of Ventonia have graduated from LLU. Many of them still keep close ties with the university.

An internal journal, “Ventonia Taurētājs”, used to be published regularly. Since 2016 the “Circular of the Philisters’ Society of Ventonia” has been published every year in its place.

The history of Ventonia is linked with the Latvian Students’ Circle of the University of Tartu, founded in 1913, and from 1915 with the Union of Latvian Students. On 9 May 1917 the union was officially turned into a fraternity, and on 20 November the name “Ventonia” was adopted. In 1919 its work was resumed in Riga, at the University of Latvia. On 21 October 1921 “Ventonia” united with another new Latvian fraternity of Tartu, “Jumeria”. The official founding date of the fraternity is 21 November 1917. It was admitted to P!K! on 1 November 1921. On 30 November 1921 it was registered as a student society of the University of Latvia, the fraternity “Ventonia”. It was closed on 13 July 1940, under the Soviet occupation, by order of the Minister of Public Affairs. It resumed its work after the Second World War, in 1945, in the refugee camps. In 1954 a Global Presidium was formed. In exile there were four chapters in the USA and one each in England, Australia, Germany, Sweden and Canada. It resumed its work on 3 February 1990 and was registered at the Latvian Academy of Agriculture (now LLU) on 7 September 1990. Since 4 May 1996 the global presidium has been in Latvia. There has been friendship with Fraternitas Liviensis (Estonia) since 1930; a cartel agreement was signed in Riga on 1 July 1995.

The Philisters’ Aid Society of Ventonia existed from 1924 and was dissolved in 1940. In 1929 it joined the F!B!S!. After 1941 the philisters’ society partly resumed its work, and on 20 December 1943 it was entered in the list of associations. It resumed its work on 13 October 1991 as the Philisters’ Society.

**Aims.** “To strive to attain the national ideals and to establish friendship and brotherhood among its members, putting first morality and the spiritual and physical development of its members.” “To gather students under its flag, to bring them up in the spirit of nation, state and the unity of the people as able citizens and public workers, to cultivate in them an awareness of duty, a sense of honour, friendship and social tact, to further interest in learning and the arts, and to further the physical development of its members through sport.”

**Motto.** “Kur spēka dīglis? – Vienībā! Spēka kodols? – Viens otra cienībā!” (Where is the seed of strength? In unity! The core of strength? In respect for one another!) “Draugam, godam, taisnībai!” (For friend, honour and justice!)

**Colours**: blue, red and gold; since 1921: blue, white and green.

**Members.** 1929: 45 commilitones, 11 members without colours and 23 philisters; 1938: 104 commilitones and 75 philisters; 1952: 90; 1977: 116; 1990: 90 members. 2009: 50 commilitones, 90 philisters, 7 members without colours and 25 philisters abroad. 2026: 135 philisters, 12 commilitones and 5 members without colours.

For bravery in the War of Independence five members of Ventonia were awarded the military Order of Lāčplēsis. The best-known members: the honorary philister General Jānis Balodis, the pastor and philister Jānis Turks, the philister and pastor M. Ķirsons, and the philisters H. Everss, Dr Georgs E. Dimza, N. Kazmers, the pastor J. Birģelis, P. Bērtulis, A. Abakuks, T. Grīnvalds, Dr Juris Gunārs-Pommers, Dr J. Ābele and V. Valainis.

**Location.** In Riga: Stabu iela 5 (1927–32), Valdemāra iela 27/29–13 (1933–40); in Jelgava: Filozofu iela 9 (since 1992).
