Fraternitas Metropolitana is the direct continuation of the fraternity Fraternitas Petropoliensis, later Fraternitas Petropolitana, which was founded by Latvian students in St Petersburg on 18 December 1896.

The founding minutes record that the society was founded on the principles by which Lettonia in Tartu and Selonija in Riga work. Fraternitas Petropolitana could not establish official relations with the other fraternities of St Petersburg, because they were in the same position as it was. Only the German fraternity Nevania had succeeded in obtaining the authorities’ permission to uncover its colours. Fraternitas Petropolitana did, however, keep up friendly relations with Lettonia, which it congratulated on the 25th anniversary of the uncovering of its colours, and with Selonija, which it congratulated on its 20th anniversary in Riga. When Talavija was founded, its first external guest was the Oldermann of Fraternitas Petropolitana.

When the revolution began, the work of Fraternitas Petropolitana broke off and its members scattered. Many fell on the battlefields; others perished in the turmoil of Russia. When the members of Petropolitana gathered in Riga on 9 September 1922, it emerged that only 17 of them were left, all graduates and not one a student. At the meeting they agreed to found the philisters’ society Fraternitas Metropolitana, with the aim of gathering the members of Petropolitana who had returned to the homeland and, in order to resume active life, of admitting students as passive members so that an active convent could be restored. On 6 October 1924, 11 students of the University of Latvia began the active life of a convent as the student fraternity Fraternitas Metropolitana of the University of Latvia, keeping as its foundation the colours of its direct predecessor (gold, violet and green), its motto (“With united strength for honour, friend and fatherland”), its traditions and its comment.

Fraternitas Metropolitana vouched for the A!K!K! with Lettgallia, and on 9 November 1926 it was admitted to the Presidium Convent, as the direct continuation in Riga of the fraternity Fraternitas Petropolitana founded in St Petersburg on 18 December 1896, with all its philisters of the St Petersburg period; but as the founding date of Fraternitas Metropolitana among the other fraternities, P!K! recognised 6 October 1924.

Fraternitas Metropolitana faced a hard dilemma: to take the easier way and accept the recognised year of 1924, or to insist on 1896, the founding year of its predecessor. The convent decided to accept 1924, so that after the long years of illegality it could the sooner begin the life of an active fraternity at the University of Latvia and take part in the work of P!K!. When Fraternitas Metropolitana was admitted to P!K!, 59 of its 98 members were philisters.

In Riga Fraternitas Metropolitana took part in the institutions of P!K!; its members held the posts of editor of Universitas and chairman of the P!K! Men’s Choir and of Universitātes Sports, and for many years the post of chairman of the Student Council. It presided over P!K! in 1929 and 1937. While Fraternitas Metropolitana presided, the 10 years of P!K! were celebrated and the P!K!T! comment and the basic rules of the fraternities’ internal life were adopted. The philisters’ society of Fraternitas Metropolitana joined the F!B!S! in 1929.

Its members took part in Latvia’s War of Independence and in the Second World War. Many fell on the battlefields or perished in deportation, in Siberia.

In exile, on 26 April 1948 in Esslingen, Germany, 38 members of Metropolitana restored the work of the convent, interrupted during the Russian and German occupations. The active convent and the Philisters’ Society united in one organisation, the United Convent of Fraternitas Metropolitana. It took part in founding L!K!A! in Esslingen and New York, and presided in 1969/70 and 1987/88. Since 20 April 1950 the convent of Fraternitas Metropolitana has had its seat in the USA. Chapters of the convent are active in the USA, Canada, Australia and Sweden. Every year the convent publishes the journal Fraternitas Metropolitana. In 1951 a new flag was consecrated, and on 10 October 1959 the restored coat of arms. The present number of members is 214, and since its founding more than 500 members have been admitted to the convent.

Fraternitas Metropolitana and its members are active in the cultural, public, national and political work of the exile community, and the fraternity is a participant in the Latvian Freedom Fund with a fourfold donation. In 1989 a memorial plaque to the freedom fighters and missing members of Fraternitas Metropolitana was unveiled at the Brothers’ Cemetery in the Catskills.
